# Spec — split the hub's inbound RPC surface by audience

**Contract.** The hub exposes its inbound RPC as *two* services, split by who
dials: `NodeIngress` — dialed by a reverse-dialing node to register itself — and
`ConsumerApi` — dialed by a consumer (the console or the agent shim) to discover
the mesh and route calls. A peer sees only the methods its role should call.
(The hub→node direction, `NodeService`, is separate and unaffected.)

**Chosen.** Audience-split services. A node is a *provider* (it reverse-dials to
offer connectors); a consumer is a *reader/router*. The split matches that role
boundary, and since vox runs each service as its own lane, the hub can accept and
authorize the two independently.

**Rejected.** One `HubService` carrying both `register` and `topology`/`route`.
It hands every peer methods outside its role — a node could call `route`, a
consumer could `register` — so the type surface no longer states who may do what,
leaving it to hub-side convention.

**Escape hatch.** The split is by *dialer*, not a hard partition of all hub RPC:
a capability genuinely needed by both audiences may be offered on both services,
or factored into a third shared service — without collapsing the two back into
one.
